Brazil has more than 22,000 catalogued caves, and licensing in mining requires measured cave maps. Today these measurements are taken with heterogeneous methods that are hard to compare or audit.

The Digital Speleometry project, which I coordinate at UFMG, is building a computational framework that automates speleometric analysis from 3D sensing and computer vision, so that cave surveys produce consistent, repeatable measurements.
